# One Shot Medium Routing

Choose the final medium from the product experience, not agent familiarity. Use more than one track for a genuinely hybrid product.

## Software and interactive products

- Prefer a locally runnable architecture with minimal setup and deterministic sample data.
- Preserve a user-specified stack when it remains viable. Otherwise choose the smallest mature stack that can deliver the complete experience and be maintained.
- Design real navigation, state, persistence, errors, accessibility, responsiveness, and security boundaries as applicable.
- Stub external services only behind explicit adapters and label their local simulation. Never present a stub as a live integration.
- Include setup, checks, build, and preview commands. Run them in a clean local environment before release.
- Defer hosting, production credentials, billing, domains, analytics enrollment, and app-store submission.

## Physical products and print

- Identify at least one current service that supports one or two units or another genuinely small run.
- Use official vendor dielines, templates, material specifications, bleed/safe-area rules, color profiles, tolerances, and export requirements.
- Record direct source URLs, vendor, template version/date, target size, and any account-gated limitation.
- Preserve downloaded original templates separately from editable working masters.
- Build print/manufacturing-ready files plus locally viewable renders or assembled mockups.
- Verify dimensions, fonts, image resolution, bleed, color space, pagination/panel order, and package contents with appropriate tools.
- Defer ordering, payment, shipping submission, or file upload. Provide a dry-run production checklist.

## Media, editorial, and content products

- Define audience, format, duration/extent, content architecture, voice, accessibility, and distribution package.
- Produce the complete master and a locally reviewable form, not an outline alone.
- Preserve editable sources and final exports. Verify captions, transcripts, links, credits, color/audio levels, dimensions, or pagination as applicable.
- Distinguish licensed source material, cited inspiration, and generated material.
- Defer public publishing, syndication, and paid distribution.

## Services, events, and experiences

- Implement the service blueprint, participant journey, operator workflow, scripts/content, forms, failure recovery, and realistic local simulation.
- Create tangible artifacts that let a reviewer experience the service without pretending an external organization exists.
- Define staffing, timing, handoffs, privacy/safety, capacity, and operating metrics.
- Defer bookings, outreach, venue commitments, purchases, or live participant contact.

## Hybrid products

- Design each medium with its native specialist workflow.
- Define the interface between media: identifiers, dimensions, data exchange, packaging, timing, and ownership.
- Build one integrated local preview that demonstrates the complete cross-medium journey.
- Do not let a polished component conceal an unfinished dependency in another medium.

## Web research and evidence

Browse whenever technology, product availability, standards, prices, vendor capabilities, or templates may have changed. Prefer primary sources: official documentation, standards bodies, manufacturers, and service providers. Use secondary inspiration for taste, not authoritative specifications.

For every production-affecting source, record the direct URL, access date, applicable version, exact constraint used, and the artifact or decision it influenced. Label inferences. Do not download or reuse copyrighted inspiration assets unless the license clearly allows the intended use.
